# H013 completion-mass gate: independent theoretical diagnosis

This is a theory and implementation-contract review of the unlabelled gate failure reported by the observer. It does not read construction references, rescore cached responses, repair the existing run, or authorize a retry.

The reported log candidate mass is +0.00051585009, so its corresponding mass is approximately 1.000516. If the two distinct full-name-plus-terminal-EOS sequences are mutually exclusive leaves under the same causal probability kernel and the same tokenized prefix, their summed unconditional leaf probability must be at most one. That inequality follows from the probability tree; it does not depend on which name is correct. The reported result therefore fails exact common-kernel coherence. Candidate-set normalization would conceal this failure and cannot repair the original unconditional probabilities.

The two reported full-vocabulary log-normalizers, 41.82256565 and 41.85320878, do not by themselves demonstrate a causal-mask or row-alignment bug. Adding a common constant to every vocabulary logit changes the log-normalizer by that constant and leaves the normalized distribution unchanged. A valid comparison would concern normalized distributions for the same history, rather than log-normalizers alone. The mass failure establishes the joint inconsistency but does not locate it.

Mathematically identical causal-prefix rows may differ under separate finite-precision executions with different candidate lengths or tensor shapes. Projection/reduction rounding, backend shape choices, incorrect prefix identity, causal alignment, attention masking, or other implementation effects are possible classes of explanation; none is uniquely established by the reported scalar audit. Float64 log-sum-exp of stored FP16 logits stabilizes the reduction but does not restore equality of two different upstream logit vectors. No conclusion about model capacity, natural reasoning, labels, or an internal attention mechanism follows from this failure.

A separate engineering qualification can structurally define one finite-precision probability tree. For every unique token history, obtain and normalize exactly one full-vocabulary next-token vector, reuse that vector for all outgoing candidate edges, and include every candidate token followed by the fixed terminal EOS. A trie over complete token sequences provides the bookkeeping. At each node, the probabilities of retained distinct vocabulary-token edges sum to at most one; induction then bounds the total retained leaf mass by one, including branches with different lengths. This guarantee concerns the numerical observation contract, not improved factuality.

The terminal-EOS convention must make candidate leaves prefix-free. EOS may occur in the pre-existing chat prefix, but cannot occur inside a candidate's name tokens. Identical token sequences must be deduplicated as events while preserving all logical references. Merely right-padding candidate sequences does not prove shared-history identity unless each shared node is actually evaluated once or its normalized distribution is checked and reused. No temperature, clipping, per-name renormalization, relaxed mass threshold, or result-dependent selection is a substitute.

The H013 thirty-two sealed child observations remain valid retained raw records of the attempted instrument, but the missing successful root completion and failed probability-coherence gate prevent them from being released as a qualified probability experiment. A trie-based instrument would have a different observation contract and must receive its own pre-observation engineering checks, source/runtime identity and frozen protocol. Existing observations and the failure must remain intact.
